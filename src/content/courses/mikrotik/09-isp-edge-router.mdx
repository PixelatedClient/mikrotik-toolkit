Most of this lesson's checklist (hardware sizing questions, the two-upstream policy, the protection checklist, the operations habits) is this project's own design guidance for running an edge router safely, not a rule quoted from MikroTik's documentation — it draws on the same BGP mechanics (distance, dynamic AS learning, instance vs template) already sourced in [Routing: Static, OSPF and BGP Intro](/learn/mikrotik/03-routing-static-ospf-bgp) and this project's own real-router BGP testing.

## The job of the edge

The edge router sits between your network and the rest of the internet. It has four responsibilities:

1. **Carry BGP** to one or more upstreams and announce your prefixes.
2. **Aggregate customers** and route traffic to them.
3. **Protect itself and you**: filters, anti-spoofing, rate limits.
4. **Stay up**: redundancy and monitoring.

The [config generator](/tools/mikrotik-config-generator) ISP edge role builds the single-upstream baseline. This lesson explains the decisions around it.

## Sizing the hardware

A router's limits are **packets per second** and **routing table size**, not just megabits.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Full table or default route only? | A full IPv4 table is roughly 1.1 million routes as of this writing (checked against the live count, which only grows over time). It needs enough RAM and CPU for convergence. |
| Peak Mbps and pps? | Small packets cost more CPU per bit. Size for pps. |
| Number of BGP sessions? | Each adds memory and update processing. |

If you cannot afford a full table, take **default route plus your upstream's customer routes**, or a partial table. Many small ISPs run on a default route from each of two upstreams.

## Loopback and router ID

Use a loopback-style address for the router ID and iBGP, so sessions survive a physical link failure:

```
/interface bridge add name=loopback
/ip address add address=192.0.2.1/32 interface=loopback
# then create a BGP instance with router-id=192.0.2.1 and your AS, and reference it from each connection
```

## Two upstreams

With two providers you need policy, not just connectivity:

- Prefer one for outbound with a higher **local-pref**.
- Announce your prefix to both, and use **AS-path prepending** on the backup so inbound prefers the primary.
- Never re-announce one upstream's routes to the other, or you become an accidental transit network. Your outbound filter (only your own prefixes) prevents that.

Details are in the Advanced Routing track: [BGP in ISP networks](/learn/routing/03-bgp-in-isp-networks).

## Customer aggregation

Customers connect by VLAN, PPPoE or routed handoff. Regardless of method:

- Give each customer a **/32 or a small block** and route it to them.
- **Summarise** in BGP: announce your /22 or /24 aggregate, not thousands of /32s.
- Enforce anti-spoofing on the customer side: drop packets whose source is not the customer's prefix.

## Protection checklist

- Input chain: drop by default; BGP only from peers; management only from a trusted list.
- BGP filters on input and output.
- Bogon and martian filtering on the upstream port — nothing claiming to originate from an RFC 1918 private range, or from your own prefixes, should ever arrive from the internet side.
- Disable unused services and neighbour discovery on the WAN.
- Rate-limit ICMP and connection attempts to the router.

## Operations

- Back up the export: `/export file=edge-backup` (then copy the file off the router).
- Monitor BGP sessions, interface errors, CPU, and the temperature.
- Keep a **console path** that does not depend on the router being healthy.
- Change on a maintenance window, and use safe mode.
