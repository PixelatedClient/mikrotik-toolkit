## Why survey before deploying

Wireless behaves differently in every building — wall material, furniture, interference sources and floor plan all change coverage in ways a floor plan alone doesn't predict. A site survey measures the real environment before AP placement is finalised, instead of guessing and finding the dead zones after installation.

## Passive vs active survey

- **Passive survey**: walk the site with a scanning tool, measuring existing signal (from neighbouring networks, if any) and noise, without transmitting from a test AP. Establishes a baseline of the RF environment before anything is installed.
- **Active survey**: place a temporary AP, then walk the site measuring the actual signal, throughput and roaming behaviour it produces, adjusting placement before committing to permanent mounting.

A predictive survey (software-modelled from a floor plan and wall materials, no physical walk-through) is faster and common for initial planning, but a physical walk-through survey catches what the model can't: real furniture, real interference sources, RF-unfriendly wall material a floor plan doesn't note.

## Coverage overlap for roaming

A client should always be in range of more than one AP near a cell edge, with enough signal overlap (commonly recommended around -67 dBm or better at the boundary) that it can roam to the next AP before the current one becomes unusable — not after. Too little overlap causes a client to hang onto a fading AP too long ("sticky client" behaviour); too much overlap wastes airtime to co-channel interference between APs that are unnecessarily both audible at full strength in the same spot.

## What makes roaming fast

Moving between APs on the same SSID should be seamless to the user — no re-typing a password, ideally no noticeable pause. This depends on:

- **Same SSID, same security settings, across every AP** — the client sees one logical network, not several to choose between.
- **Fast roaming standards** (802.11r for fast BSS transition, 802.11k for neighbour reports, 802.11v for AP-assisted client steering) let the client and infrastructure cooperate on when and where to roam, instead of the client blindly re-scanning.
- **Controller-based or centrally managed APs** (rather than fully independent standalone APs) typically coordinate channel and power plans automatically and support these fast-roaming standards more consistently.

## Putting it together

A good deployment plan: survey first (predictive for initial placement, then a walk-through to verify), place APs for enough overlap at cell edges for roaming without excessive co-channel interference in the middle, and match channel/power settings across APs so a moving client always has a clearly better next AP to roam to rather than several equally weak ones to hesitate between.

## Check yourself

- What does an active survey measure that a passive survey does not?
- Why does too much coverage overlap between APs hurt performance, not just too little?
- What does same SSID and security settings across every AP actually buy you for roaming?
